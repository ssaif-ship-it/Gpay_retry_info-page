A mistyped UPI PIN is one of the few payment failures that has nothing to do with whether the customer could actually pay. The balance is there, the account is linked, and the customer had already decided to buy. They pressed the wrong digit.

Google Pay accounts for this. It offers the customer another attempt inside the app, on the same screen, without sending them back to checkout.

The catch is that by the time that prompt appears, the transaction has usually already been closed.

What Happens on a Wrong PIN

The flow is simple in concept:

  1. The customer picks UPI at checkout and selects Google Pay.
  2. Google Pay opens, the customer confirms the account and enters their UPI PIN.
  3. If the PIN is wrong, the bank rejects the authorisation and returns a failure.
  4. Google Pay shows a wrong PIN message with the option to try again.

Step 4 is the part most businesses never see the benefit of. The failure from step 3 comes back through the gateway in a couple of seconds and the transaction is marked failed and terminal right there, so the order is already closed on the merchant side while the customer is still looking at a live retry button on their phone. When they tap it, there is nothing left for that retry to complete against.

Actual Google Pay screens from a live UPI checkout. What changes with Order Retry is not this screen, it is whether the order behind it is still open.

The Timing Gap

This is not a decline in any real sense. Nothing about the customer or the account failed a check. A recoverable input error gets treated as a final answer because of the order in which two systems happen to respond.

For the customer, it looks like a payment that broke for no reason.

For the business, it lands in the success rate looking identical to a genuine insufficient funds or bank downtime failure, and whatever support ticket follows has no useful resolution.

Our Solution: GPay Order Retry

Cashfree has worked with Google Pay to keep the order open through the retry window, so that Google Pay's own retry prompt has something to complete against.

Instead of treating a wrong PIN failure as terminal the moment it arrives, Cashfree checks with Google whether the transaction is still eligible for a retry. If it is, the transaction is held in a non terminal state for a short window while the customer takes a second attempt.

With this live:

  • The customer gets Google Pay's standard retry prompt and enters the correct PIN in the same session, with no need to return to checkout or restart the payment.
  • The retry completes against the original order. No duplicate transaction is created and no second charge is possible.
  • If the retry succeeds, the payment moves to success as normal, with no extra step for the merchant or the customer.
  • If it does not, the transaction resolves as a failure exactly as it would have before.

Eligibility is checked per transaction and defaults to no. Anything that cannot be confirmed as retryable is treated as a standard failure, so the old behaviour is the fallback in every uncertain case.
